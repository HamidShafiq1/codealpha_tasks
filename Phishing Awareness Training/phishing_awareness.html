WHAT IS PHISHING?

⚡ Definition

Phishing is a type of social engineering attack where adversaries impersonate trusted entities to deceive victims into revealing sensitive information — credentials, financial data, or personally identifiable information (PII).

The name derives from "fishing" — attackers cast a wide net hoping victims will "take the bait."

🎯 Types of Phishing

  • ►
    Email Phishing — Mass emails impersonating banks, services, or HRMOST COMMON
  • ►
    Spear Phishing — Targeted attack on specific individual with personalized info
  • ►
    Whaling — Targets C-level executives (CEO, CFO)HIGH VALUE
  • ►
    Smishing — Phishing via SMS text messages
  • ►
    Vishing — Voice/phone call phishing
  • ►
    Clone Phishing — Replicates a legitimate email with malicious links

📊 By the Numbers

  • ■ 36% of all data breaches involve phishing (Verizon DBIR 2023)
  • ■ 3.4 billion phishing emails sent per day globally
  • ■ Average cost of phishing attack on mid-size company: $1.6M
  • ■ 97% of users cannot identify a sophisticated phishing email

RECOGNIZING PHISHING EMAILS

🔍 Anatomy of a Phishing Email

Hover over the highlighted red elements in the email below to reveal why each is suspicious.

From: security@paypa1.com
To: user@gmail.com
Subject: ⚠ URGENT: Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!

Dear Valued Customer,


We have detected unusual activity on your account. To avoid permanent suspension, you must verify your details immediately.


Click the button below to secure your account:

Verify My Account Now

If you do not verify within 24 hours, your account will be permanently closed.


Regards,
PayPal Security Team

🚨 Red Flags Checklist

  • ✗ Mismatched or misspelled sender domain
  • ✗ Urgent language creating panic (24 hours, IMMEDIATELY)
  • ✗ Generic greetings ("Dear Customer" instead of your name)
  • ✗ Requests for credentials, SSN, or payment info
  • ✗ Hover URL doesn't match displayed link text
  • ✗ Poor grammar or unusual formatting
  • ✗ Unexpected attachments (.exe, .zip, .docm)
  • ✗ Requests to bypass normal security procedures

FAKE WEBSITES & SOCIAL ENGINEERING

🌐 How to Spot Fake Websites

  • ►
    URL Inspection — Check for typosquatting: paypa1.com, arnazon.com, g00gle.com
  • ►
    HTTPS ≠ Safe — Padlock only means encrypted transit, NOT that the site is legitimate
  • ►
    Domain Age — Use whois.domaintools.com to check if domain is newly registered
  • ►
    Redirect Chains — Legitimate sites rarely redirect you through multiple domains
  • ►
    Cloned UI — Pixel-perfect copies of real sites; look for missing nav links, broken pages

🧠 Social Engineering Tactics

  • ► Authority — Impersonates IT, HR, CEO, bank, or government
  • ► Urgency / Scarcity — Time-limited threats to bypass rational thinking
  • ► Fear — "Your account has been compromised" / "You owe taxes"
  • ► Reciprocity — Offers a "free gift" to lower defenses
  • ► Social Proof — "Other users have already verified their accounts"
  • ► Liking — Builds rapport before making malicious request
⚠ CRITICAL RULE: No legitimate organization will ever ask for your password, OTP, or full card number via email or phone. Period.

✅ Best Practices

  • ✓ Always navigate directly to websites — never click email links
  • ✓ Enable MFA on all accounts (authenticator app, not SMS)
  • ✓ Use a password manager — prevents credential reuse
  • ✓ Verify unexpected requests through a separate channel (call back on official number)
  • ✓ Keep browser and OS updated — patches known phishing bypass exploits
  • ✓ Use email filtering tools (Proofpoint, Mimecast) in enterprise environments
  • ✓ Report phishing attempts to your IT/security team immediately
